## P2: Quiesce the whole owned process group and keep restoration/evidence independent of shutdown errors

Location: `cost-supervise.py:20–36`.

The supervisor correctly creates a new owned session/process group, but on a deadline, disk floor or exception it sends group TERM and then waits **only for the bash leader**. If bash exits promptly while an owned cargo/compiler/test descendant survives TERM, `p.wait()` succeeds and the KILL branch is skipped. The descendant can continue using resources or writing fixture/build files after the selected stop and while source restoration is reported complete. Reaping a group leader is not evidence that the group has stopped. The explicit whole-owned-group TERM/grace/KILL policy must not depend solely on leader liveness.

Also, group signaling or either wait can raise inside the `finally` (notably the second ten-second `p.wait` timeout). That exception skips all later source hash restoration and `cost-completion.json` persistence. `source.read_bytes`, restoration and disk-accounting errors likewise escape before a completion receipt. This undermines the experiment's explicit exact-restoration/error-evidence boundary. The initial normal `Popen` exception path is handled, but these cleanup paths are not.

Use a bounded stop of the **known owned group**, with group-liveness/status handling after TERM and final KILL if needed even when the leader has exited. Reap the leader separately; record unresolved termination rather than claiming quiescence. Keep source verification/restoration and completion-receipt attempts in independently protected cleanup stages, so a shutdown error cannot skip them. Preserve unexpected source bytes instead of overwriting them. Do not broaden signaling to names, unrelated PIDs or unrelated groups. A small supervisor correction suffices; no product source, test or general cleanup framework change is needed. The lead should inspect the corrected exact supervisor/hash before launch.
