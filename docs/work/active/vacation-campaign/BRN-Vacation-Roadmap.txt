3. THE ROADMAP: DEPENDENCY GATES, WITH A TWO-WEEK PLANNING ENVELOPE

Days are indicative allocations, not promises about throughput or unlimited credit.
Advance by evidence. Defects and journey tests belong inside each slice; the later
hardening phase does not postpone correctness. Reserve the final part of the
campaign for integration, evidence and handoff instead of starting broad new work.
The lead may overlap or reorder work according to dependencies and current findings.
These milestones define required outcomes; named candidates and the suggested
implementation sequence may change under the campaign's engineering authority.

G1 — Linux execution and the full workflow inventory (roughly days 1–2).

Run the production application and current component tests on Linux. Establish
which display, renderer, input mechanism, fonts and synthetic workspace actually
work. A compile-only or no-window driver is not sufficient. In parallel, inventory
all BRN-owned workflows and real action routes, plus test-family obligations and
feedback costs. Begin current helper and atomic storage portability in independently
owned work. Keep this audit bounded and apply findings inside implementation slices.

Gate: the workflow denominator is explicit; component tests really execute; one
real Linux window journey has qualified rendering/input/saved outcomes, or the
precise environmental blocker is recorded. Independent reducer work may continue
when a graphics blocker is genuine, but the Linux goal remains open.

G2 — Prove the state ownership pattern in production (roughly days 2–3).

Choose a high-risk workflow with input, async completion and a durable effect. The
current writing/setup/run/review seams are strong candidates; a separate notice
cleanup is optional. Define the independent contract, model states/events/effects, wire actual
callbacks, protect stale/cancelled results and retire the replaced route and its
mechanism tests. Reuse the real WritingPanel host and review the pilot before rollout.

Gate: a reviewer accepts responsibility placement; relevant state/sequence checks and actual
production interaction exercise the same workflow and verify its user outcome.
Typed events without state ownership, or model tests with old callbacks still
mutating state, do not pass.

G3 — Complete all remaining BRN-owned workflow domains (roughly days 3–8).

Migrate in bounded vertical slices, ordered by current defects and dependencies.
Each slice includes its state model, service boundary, production wiring, relevant
Linux evidence, risk-selected behavioral checks and obsolete-path retirement. Reuse
applicable existing witnesses; demonstrate representative production interaction
when wiring changes, and choose sequence/failure cases according to the risk.
Test interactions between domains as well as individual screens. Update the manifest
whenever the inventory expands.

Gate: every discovered BRN-owned stateful workflow has one qualified owner; stateless
and toolkit/platform-owned cases are justified; no unexplained duplicate mutation
route remains. An incomplete migration stays visibly incomplete at expiry.

G4 — Strategic programming and reliability hardening (roughly days 8–11).

Address repeated causes exposed by the journeys: caller coordination, duplicated
policy, unclear async ownership, recovery gaps, unsafe platform boundaries and
measured responsiveness and feedback-cost problems. Strengthen deep modules and reuse
pinned libraries. Consolidate qualified provider fixtures/CI work and remove replaced
production paths and tests after preserving unique behavior. Keep cleanup tied to
a demonstrated engineering or user benefit. [5]

Gate: critical invariants have evidence, actionable release-blocking findings are
resolved, and simplification reduces what callers must know. There is no quota
for deleted lines, abstraction layers, dependency upgrades or mutation scores.

G5 — Exact candidate, complete journeys and release handoff (roughly days 12–14).

Run the integrated critical-journey matrix, relevant platform and required CI gates,
selected crash/recovery scenarios, package relocation/startup, supported upgrades
and backup/restore. Review the final integrated commit independently; material
fixes require their affected checks and appropriate re-review. Prepare release
notes, candidate artifacts and an evidence-linked readiness report.

Gate: engineering release criteria pass on the recorded candidate, with no known
release-blocking defect and all outstanding contractual platform/live/owner gates
stated accurately. Deliver a draft PR and candidate; do not publish or merge main.
If a gate remains open when time or useful capacity ends, preserve the coherent
partial result and specific blockers instead of claiming readiness.

5. LINUX ACCEPTANCE IS SEVERAL DISTINCT QUESTIONS

A. Build and component interaction.
Use locked repository dependencies and current CI commands. Qualify native UI and
writing-host/widget suites on Linux. Confirm tests execute and real controls drive
the app's callbacks. Preserve Mac lanes. A zero-test filter or skipped suite does
not establish behavior.

B. Production window, pixels and input.
Run the actual application in a qualified graphical session. Prove rendering,
real input and an edit/save/reopen outcome. Xvfb is only a display option; it does
not itself provide a suitable renderer. GPUI component snapshots inspect behavior
and layout, not necessarily pixels or packaged application startup. Retain a real
window lane for those claims. [6]

C. Current Xberg helpers and storage.
Implement Linux restrictions at the current helper_runtime boundary, preserving
both helper modes and their intended isolation, resource limits and cancellation.
Do not turn restrictions off to obtain a green Linux run. Qualify atomic no-replace
model publication on Linux and cleanup under contention/failure using synthetic
payloads. Keep the retired Poppler pipeline retired. [3]

D. Desktop behavior.
Check Linux font fallback, key bindings and labels, Unicode text, Tab traversal,
focus restoration, dialogs, scrolling, resize and visible errors/progress. Exercise
these with real data outcomes. A screenshot without input or a synthetic layout
snapshot alone is not the completed Linux user experience.

E. Distribution scope.
Linux capability for development/UI testing is the immediate goal. Claim Linux
release distribution only after its actual packaging/install/update obligations
are qualified. Linux evidence does not satisfy a genuinely Mac-specific gate.

6. AUTONOMOUS UX TESTING WITHOUT MULTIPLYING THE SUITE

The earlier eight methods are a selection menu, not eight mandatory suites per
workflow, platform, feature and provider. Assign each consequential guarantee one
authoritative behavioral interface. Add another form of evidence only for a distinct
failure it can catch. Reuse current witnesses first and retire replaced mechanism
tests in the same change. Complete UI ownership does not imply exhaustive testing
of the application's entire state cross-product.

Use the smallest useful choice for the risk:

- Pure decision: examples or a transition table; use generated/shrunk sequences when
  ordering or combinations warrant it. The independent expectation must not call the
  production reducer to compute its own answer. Save minimal regressions. [7]
- Async completion: control order and time to expose old search/save/retry results,
  cancellation and wrong-document/project effects. Use pinned test facilities.
- New UI binding: one representative real control-to-operation-to-view journey;
  retain a distinct keyboard path if its binding is separate. Keep the real writing
  host and inspect durable outcomes when relevant. Pure state tests miss wiring.
- Equivalent routes: compare saved outcomes for toolbar/menu/shortcut, valid stream
  chunking and cancel-with-late-result where the contract promises equivalence. [8]
- Persistence/OS behavior: selected real store, process, lock, signal, helper and
  crash/reopen witnesses. Controllable decision tests can reduce needless waits,
  but do not erase the real integration behavior being qualified.
- Pixels/package: selected real-window and exact-package witnesses on affected
  platforms. Headless layout and semantic checks prove different facts. [6, 11]
- Suspicious assertion: a bounded mutation experiment on that guard, with meaningful
  survivors/equivalence assessed rather than a target score. [10]
- Major milestone: a few independent goal-only UI exploration tasks if actual native
  control is available. Give a synthetic profile and a goal without source/click
  instructions; verify outcomes separately through supported read-only operations.
  The actor cannot bypass the UI or repair the fixture with CLI/SQL. [9]

Examples remain practical: edit/start save/edit again/switch/old completion/reopen;
Stop followed by a late result; import and find a document; recover a draft; inspect
an exact candidate, Apply and Undo. Vary keyboard use, mistakes, Unicode, interruptions
and window size where relevant. Do not generate every combination by default.

For a costly new test, identify the defect class existing checks miss, the appropriate
interface and its bounded execution cost. Record the exact build/platform, synthetic
fixture, minimal actions, seed or completion/fault schedule and useful first-divergence
image/log when a defect occurs. A separate checker should inspect the saved outcome,
not merely accept an actor's “looks good.” Turn expensive discoveries into cheap
regressions and stop when the concrete risk has adequate evidence.

Track goal completion, dead ends, unnecessary actions, focus loss, keyboard access,
clipping, progress/errors and recovery. Measure real release-build latency separately
from fake-time logic. Automated evidence can reveal usability defects; it cannot
certify human intuition/comfort or native screen-reader behavior from semantics alone.
Do not introduce a new agent platform, test framework or blanket dependency bundle.

7. CRITICAL RELEASE JOURNEY MATRIX

Each journey needs a fixture, real action routes, expected visible outcome, expected
durable outcome, failure/recovery variant, applicable platform and exact build/SHA.

J1 — Fresh first use and edit/save/reopen.
A new supported configuration opens, explains its empty state and lets the user
create meaningful saved content. Close and reopen it. Verify Unicode and a meaningful
keyboard path. No false success or lost newer draft.

J2 — Import to usable information.
Use representative qualified inputs across the current conversion modes and format
inventory. Observe progress, cancel/error behavior and useful saved content/search.
Preserve AI conversion Off by default. Record unsupported/encrypted or fidelity
limits instead of claiming universal conversion.

J3 — Scoped agent writing through reviewed Apply and Undo.
Choose project/references, run with controlled provider behavior, inspect the exact
candidate, Apply to the intended revision and Undo as specified. Cover stop/retry/
resume, stale candidates, wrong scope, later edits and a late completion. The service
must enforce the same rules for every caller.

J4 — Navigate while work is in flight.
Change document/project or search query while saving, converting, retrieving or
running. The completion reaches its intended owner or is rejected; it must not
change the new view's target. Check useful feedback and retained work.

J5 — Existing capabilities and understandable errors.
Exercise relevant settings, connection/provider setup, skills/MCP, task/history and
archive/Trash/restore flows present after #159. Use controlled external clients.
Distinguish offline/mock evidence from actual live service behavior.

J6 — Backup, upgrade, restart and recovery.
Use supported old-schema fixtures, including the known schema-7 witness gap if still
applicable. Upgrade with backup first; restore to a fresh directory; verify supported
older-binary refusal and committed data. Exercise index rebuild and selected crash/
I/O failure boundaries. Derive current schema and exact obligations from source.

J7 — Exact candidate package.
Build using existing release machinery, relocate/install into a fresh supported
configuration, open the actual app and verify helper discovery plus representative
J1/J2/J3 outcomes. Fixture drivers alone do not qualify the graphical application.
Keep applicable Mac/package/owner/live acceptance distinct. [4]

8. ENGINEERING AUTONOMY AND STRATEGIC PROGRAMMING

The lead may discover, choose, design, implement, verify, review and integrate useful
improvements without repeated owner approval. A task is eligible when a defect,
review finding, source-backed simplification, usability friction, or a measurable
reliability, performance or feedback-cost opportunity identifies a concrete benefit.
It need not already be a backlog item. Prefer fixing the owning module once over
adding coordination to every caller. Preserve checked services, local ownership,
useful library reuse and data compatibility. Retire the replaced mechanism after
its replacement is demonstrated. [5]

This includes justified module rewrites, internal design changes, removal of obsolete
code and redundant tests, appropriate dependency/tooling changes, and improvements
to usability, recovery, diagnostics, CI and engineering guidance. Choose the smallest
coherent change that solves the problem, with a larger rewrite eligible when keeping
the design would preserve the demonstrated problem. Briefly explain consequential
changes to the plan in the existing work record. Use bounded experiments and keep
or revert them according to evidence. The lead and reviewer decide routine tradeoffs.

For every structural change answer: what problem becomes harder to cause; which
module owns the decision; what callers no longer need to know; what old path was
removed; what user outcome stayed correct or improved; and what evidence supports it.

Connect new work to the agreed first-release experience. Preserve product direction,
required checks, compatibility, data integrity and truthful evidence. Keep UI/Linux
progress and release-blocking defects ahead of unrelated expansion. After each
accepted result, choose the next valuable eligible improvement and continue within
the budget and deadline. Blocked tasks retain a checkpoint while independent work
continues. The owner receives progress and consequential decision records rather
than an approval request for every implementation choice.

Engineering discretion includes campaign-branch/draft-PR integration. Main merging,
publication, new spending and access beyond existing authorization remain owner-
reserved. This authority does not create missing credentials, tool capabilities or
persistent hosting. Prepare accessible work, report a concrete capability gap, and
continue independent tasks; claim unattended operation only after it is qualified.

For a consequential uncertain problem, compare materially different approaches:
remove the source of complexity, deepen an existing owner, reuse a proven facility,
or revise a design that retains the defect. A small diagnostic/prototype should
separate useful hypotheses. Record expected benefit, disconfirming evidence and a
budget before expensive experiments; keep/revise/revert based on the result. Obvious
small fixes do not require a formal alternatives exercise.

Research first in current contracts, callers and pinned dependencies. Before a
consequential decision based on uncertain external facts, use official docs, upstream
source, release notes or primary research for the actual platform/version. Check
license, maintenance and cost where material. Save the decisive sources and distinguish
facts from inference. Use a small compatibility probe if needed. Reuse still-valid
research, stop when the decision is supported, and keep private source/data out of
public queries. These rules enable inventive fixes without endless research.

9. MODEL, SUBAGENT AND CONTEXT POLICY

This is the proposed campaign policy, not a claim about automatic product behavior.
Select the cheapest model/effort that reliably achieves the task including its
verification. Use actual available settings and observed results. [18, 19]

Lead and substantial workers — GPT-6.1 Sol.
Start High for initial cross-domain planning, architecture and consequential
integration review. Use Medium for routine coordination and ordinary tasks when
adequate. The lead can implement a small change directly when delegation would add
more overhead than value. Do not keep an expensive manager generating idle status.

Bounded workers — GPT-6 Luna.
Use for focused source exploration, CI/log analysis, primary-source research,
straightforward fixes, fixture consolidation and documentation with clear acceptance.
Start High when offered, lowering effort where evidence supports it. Luna can deliver
real implementation, not merely clerical work. Escalate when interacting constraints
or unresolved evidence make the cheap attempt poor value.

Difficult escalation — GPT-6 Astra.
Use a bounded consultation or independent review for difficult ambiguity, a serious
cross-domain tradeoff or a blocker Sol has not resolved. Send the precise question,
evidence and failed hypotheses, then return implementation to Sol/Luna when useful.
Do not require Astra review or Max/Ultra reasoning for every task. Prefer Standard
speed for unattended work; task-specific delegation does not require Ultra.

Verify child model/effort rather than assuming a role label changes them. Unset
children can inherit the parent, and custom-agent configuration can affect routing.
Availability and controls depend on the client. Do not invent Cloud flags, physical
context-window controls or paid API fallbacks. Current model guidance/rates inform
routing; observed account allowance governs admission. [18, 19]

A worker is worthwhile when it improves accepted throughput, useful independence
or decision quality after context, review and integration overhead. Start with one
worker plus a reviewer when needed. Add concurrent workers only for independent
ownership with agreed interfaces and sufficient compute/allowance. Reuse relevant
worker context; use a fresh small brief for unrelated work when supported. Avoid
multiple agents solving the same task or recursively spawning review teams.

Task brief (usually 200–600 words plus paths): ID, exact baseline/branch, one outcome,
allowed files/ownership, relevant contracts/invariants, small source map, acceptance
and focused commands, expected deliverable and budget/stop condition. Include all
applicable instructions. Workers do not need the entire campaign history.

Result (usually 200–400 words plus evidence): outcome, SHA/files, consequential
decision, actual check command/configuration/result, uncertainty and next action.
Keep raw logs/patches in files and link them. These are adjustable communication
budgets, not model window limits. The lead inspects relevant diffs and validates
important claims without importing every worker transcript.

Maintain one source/test-command map and a compact checkpoint at milestones and
before crowded/stale context or handoff. It must preserve campaign/expiry, branch/SHA,
current goal, accepted and pending changes, invariants, ownership, decisive evidence,
useful failed hypotheses, active jobs, checks, blockers and next task. Detailed logs
and historical alternatives stay in evidence files. Reconcile actual Git/remote
state after compaction or fresh continuation. Do not repeatedly restart a relevant
worker merely to make its context small; repeated setup also costs.

Use selective file reads, narrow searches, mechanical log summaries and supported
compaction while leaving verification/handoff headroom. Full physical window
capacity is not a target amount of content to load. Track retry/duplicate work and
obvious context churn lightly; do not create an accounting framework.

10. GOAL, CREDIT, CONTINUATION AND DAILY DECISIONS

Use the charter's compact /goal text if the exact execution surface supports it.
The official docs name desktop, interactive CLI and IDE surfaces; do not assume
command support inside every Codex Cloud task. Keep the charter as durable guidance
and use milestone completion evidence. Give ordinary workers bounded tasks, reserving
worker goals for investigations that benefit from multi-turn iteration. [20]

A thread goal does not establish cross-task persistence or automatic resumption at
an allowance reset. Honor pause/clear and budget controls; checkpoint when stopped
and use supported user/system resumption. Never spawn replacements to evade a pause
or budget. Qualify actual lifecycle/reset behavior within the existing environment.
If /goal is absent, the ordinary Cloud prompt still defines the campaign. [20]

The lead handles judgments. A small persistent non-LLM supervisor handles merge/
expiry gates, fresh account readings, timers, job reconciliation and exclusive
launches. Qualify existing native facilities first; use a minimal adapter on an
already authorized persistent host only if needed. A transient shell or successful
first Cloud task does not prove two-week hosting.

Validate the actual installed account/rate-limit and Cloud launch/list interfaces.
Use all relevant windows and observed reset timestamps; refresh before admission
because other sessions can spend allowance. Do not infer precise subscription
credits from token totals or assume fixed meanings for quota-window names. The
Cloud CLI is experimental, so qualify actual version, result delivery and supported
controls instead of inventing budget/cancel/deduplication flags. [12, 13]

Reserve enough capacity for in-flight work, review and recovery. An initial 75–80% large-job admission threshold is adjustable,
not a command to waste the rest. Smaller useful tasks can use remaining capacity
when obligations are covered. Wait for normal resets without buying credits or
changing billing settings. Recheck the meter after the reset; the clock alone is
not proof that work can resume.

Persist task dependencies, launch intent, remote IDs, commits, quota observations,
retries, blockers and next wake. Missing telemetry pauses credit-aware admission.
A lost launch response requires reconciliation before retry, not a speculative
second writer. Test duplicate wake-ups, restart, depletion/reset, multiple windows,
uncertain launch, goal pause/budget controls and expiry using synthetic state before
activation. Prove a real small Cloud round trip and fresh continuation.

Keep supervisor qualification bounded. If a required capability is absent, prepare
the concrete adapter/deployment handoff and report unattended continuation inactive.
Continue independently executable work in the current post-merge Cloud task.

The lead may split/reorder tasks, choose appropriate existing libraries, repair
reproduced defects, adjust test scope and add demonstrated release-readiness gaps.
After two unsuccessful repair cycles, reassess the hypothesis, evidence, remaining
cost and alternatives. Continue with a materially different evidence-backed approach
when justified; otherwise preserve the reproducer and choose independent work.
At expiry stop new dispatch, retain in-flight work accurately and produce the final
handoff. Do one bounded backlog review before
stopping early when no valuable eligible work remains.

11. ACCEPTANCE AND FINAL HANDOFF

Primary target: every inventoried BRN-owned stateful workflow has a qualified owner,
Linux supports meaningful application interaction and the critical release journeys
are qualified on the final integrated candidate. Time estimates are a planning
envelope; they do not lower these definitions or guarantee completion in two weeks.

The scorecard must report:

- Actual #161 merge SHA, campaign baseline and final integrated/reviewed commit.
- Workflow denominator, justified stateless/delegated entries and each remaining gap.
- Test-family dispositions, retired mechanisms and preserved unique obligations.
- Comparable compile/setup/test-body and total feedback cost, with cache/feature scope.
- Critical journeys passed/failed/blocked, with concrete visible and saved outcomes.
- Linux build/component/window/helper/storage evidence separately.
- Persistence, supported upgrade, backup/restore and exact-package qualification.
- Required CI and independent review of the final candidate, including material fixes.
- Unresolved defects, genuine platform/live/owner gates and documented product limits.
- Branch/draft PR, release notes, regression/evidence locations and next decisions.
- Actual goal/supervisor status, expiry, quota/reset observations and in-flight tasks.
- Material model-routing/escalation choices, repeated work and context-handoff issues.

A daily status should emphasize what user outcome improved, what remains blocked
and why, and the next useful task. Avoid “100% tested,” “release ready” or “running
for two weeks” when the corresponding gates have not been demonstrated.
